# Triceratops companion art brief

Use the supplied reference image as a **style reference only**. Do not recreate its characters, costumes, poses, layout, or background.

## Shared direction

Create an original cute baby triceratops for a mobile study companion game. It should have a large rounded head, tiny rounded body, three small horns, a scalloped frill, short legs, expressive glossy eyes, and a warm, friendly expression. Use a hand-drawn 2D game-sticker style: thick deep-plum outlines, soft cel shading, gentle highlight strokes, rounded proportions, and a small soft oval shadow beneath its feet. Use mint green skin, a pale cream belly and horns, rosy cheeks, and a restrained peach-pink accent. Keep the same character design, scale, camera angle, line weight, palette, and ground shadow in every image. Center a single full-body character in a square 1024 × 1024 canvas. No text, logos, borders, scenery, other characters, costumes, or props. Use a transparent background if supported; otherwise use a perfectly solid bright green background for later keying.

## Master character

Use the attached reference image plus the shared direction:

> Create the master asset for an original mint-green baby triceratops companion. It is standing front-facing with a slight three-quarter turn, smiling gently, with all four feet visible. The creature is cute, clean, polished, and readable at small mobile size. Preserve transparent background and the small oval ground shadow. This is the canonical design reference for all later stages.

## Egg stages

Generate these as a single family after the master character is approved. The egg should be pale cream with mint-green freckles and a soft peach-pink lower shading, matching the creature palette.

1. **Egg, reward 0**
   > Create a closed triceratops egg in the established companion style. It is centered, rounded, pale cream with mint-green freckles and a small oval shadow. No cracks.

2. **Egg, reward 1**
   > Edit the established triceratops egg. Preserve every colour, line, position, scale, and shadow. Add one small clean branching crack near the upper third. The crack is a thin deep-plum line with a soft warm highlight shadow.

3. **Egg, reward 2**
   > Edit the established triceratops egg. Preserve the exact egg and canvas. Extend the branching crack toward the middle and add a subtle warm glow from inside. Do not reveal the creature yet.

4. **Egg, reward 3**
   > Edit the established triceratops egg. Preserve the exact egg and canvas. Make the shell visibly split open across the upper half, with a stronger mint glow and the tiny suggestion of a mint-green frill inside. Keep the egg recognisable.

5. **Hatch, reward 4**
   > Create the hatching moment for the established baby triceratops. The same character is emerging from the cracked egg, looking delighted. Keep the egg fragments compact around its feet, retain the same centered scale and oval shadow, and use no confetti or scenery.

## Growth stages

Use the approved master character as an image reference for every stage. Change only the listed growth details.

1. **Level 1 — hatchling**: small, slightly oversized head, shy smile.
2. **Level 2 — curious**: 8% larger, brighter eyes, a tiny leaf tucked behind one frill edge.
3. **Level 3 — playful**: 16% larger, more confident stance, two tiny frill decorations, still no clothing.
4. **Level 4 — thriving**: 24% larger, warm proud smile, slightly fuller frill and a subtle peach neckerchief.
5. **Level 5 — grown companion**: 32% larger, confidently standing, refined peach neckerchief, still clearly the same baby triceratops.

For each level use:

> Edit the supplied master triceratops reference. Preserve its identity, face, palette, outline weight, camera angle, transparent background, and oval shadow exactly. Apply only the specified growth-stage change. Return one centered square game asset with no text, scenery, logo, or extra characters.

## Acceptance checks

- The master, egg stages, hatch, and every level must read as one character family at 96 px tall.
- Do not alter the line colour, body proportions, camera angle, or canvas framing between stages.
- Reject outputs with a background, text, props, duplicated limbs, malformed horns, or an inconsistent silhouette.
